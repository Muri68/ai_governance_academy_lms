Artificial intelligence is transforming the workplace at extraordinary speed. Organisations are adopting AI to work more efficiently, make better decisions and unlock new opportunities. But while investment in AI has accelerated, investment in understanding how to use it responsibly hasn't always kept pace.

The consequences are becoming increasingly clear. Organisations around the world are facing regulatory action, financial penalties and reputational damage because AI has been implemented without the right governance, oversight or expertise.

AI Governance Academy was born from that challenge.

We began by helping organisations strengthen their AI governance, reduce regulatory risk and build more responsible ways of developing and deploying AI. Through that work, we recognised something important: responsible AI isn't created by policies alone. It's created by the people making decisions every day.

That's why we launched AI Governance Academy. Knowing how to use AI is rapidly becoming an expected workplace skill. Knowing how to use it responsibly is what will distinguish tomorrow's professionals.

Why It Matters

Learning That Goes Beyond Compliance

Responsible AI is about far more than understanding legislation or avoiding regulatory action. It's about recognising risks before they become problems, asking better questions, making informed decisions and building trust in the technology you use every day.

📊

Meta — GDPR Violation

€1.2 Billion Fine

Unlawful transfers of personal data resulted in one of the largest GDPR fines in history, demonstrating the severe consequences of inadequate data governance.

🤖

OpenAI — Data Protection

Regulatory Action

Italy's data protection authority fined OpenAI over the collection and processing of personal data by ChatGPT, highlighting privacy concerns in AI systems.

👥

iTutorGroup — AI Bias

Settlement Reached

AI-powered recruitment software automatically rejected older job applicants, leading to an EEOC settlement and underscoring the risks of algorithmic bias.

Different organisations. Different technologies. The same lesson.

Responsible AI starts long before a regulator becomes involved. It starts with the decisions people make every day. That's why organisations increasingly need professionals who understand not only how AI works, but how it should be used responsibly.
